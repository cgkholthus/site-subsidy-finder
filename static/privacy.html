Privacy & disclaimers

Plain-language privacy and the fine print.

What this site collects (very little), where your searches and questions go, and the limits of what this student prototype can tell you.

Last updated November 17, 2026. Written in plain language by a student and not reviewed by a lawyer.

The short version: there are no accounts, no cookies, no ads and no tracking. Addresses you look up go to free public map services, and chatbot questions go to Google's Gemini AI. Don't type personal or confidential information into the site.

Privacy policy

What we don't collect

  • No accounts, names, emails or passwords.
  • No cookies, advertising or analytics trackers.
  • Nothing you type is saved to a database or sold to anyone.

Addresses you look up

When you search for a site, the address goes to this site's server, which looks it up with free public map services: the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder, HUD's mapping services, the National Park Service, and the City of New Orleans and City of Portland map services. Address suggestions send the partial address you've typed to the two cities' address services. Each of those services has its own privacy practices.

The server keeps recent lookups in short-term memory so repeat searches are faster. That memory isn't written to disk, and it's cleared whenever the server restarts, which on our free hosting happens regularly. Our server's own logs record only which page or feature was used, not the address you searched.

Project answers and the capital stack

Answers to "About your project" are sent to our server only to check them against program rules; they aren't stored there. They're kept in the page's web address so you can bookmark or share results. Anyone you send that link to will see them. The capital stack and pro forma calculations run in your own browser, and the spreadsheet download is created there too. Numbers you type into the capital stack page aren't sent to our server, except for a site address you choose to load.

The question assistant

When you ask the chatbot a question, your question, the conversation so far, and the site address and project answers you're viewing are sent to Google's Gemini API to write the answer. This site uses Gemini's free tier. Under Google's terms for unpaid services, Google may use what's submitted to improve its products, and people at Google may review it. Please don't include personal, financial or confidential deal information in your questions.

Other services your browser connects to

  • Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap.
  • The map library loads from cdnjs (Cloudflare).
  • Fonts load from Google Fonts.

These services receive your IP address when your browser requests their files, as with any website.

Hosting and abuse protection

The site is hosted on Render, which may keep its own technical records of requests, such as IP addresses and times. To keep the free services from being overloaded, our server briefly remembers each visitor's IP address in memory to limit how many lookups and questions can be made per minute and per day. That record expires within a day and is never saved.

Children

This site is meant for adults working in or learning about housing development. It isn't directed to children under 13.

Changes and questions

If these practices change, this page will be updated with a new date. Questions? See the About page for how to get in touch.

It doesn't recommend tax positions, investments or deal structures. Talk to a qualified attorney, CPA, tax credit syndicator or lender before making decisions.

Eligibility is never guaranteed

"Potentially relevant" means a site's location fits a program. "Matches key requirements" means your project answers also fit its published rules. Neither one means a project is eligible, will be selected or will be funded. The agency that runs each program makes the final decision. Contact it before relying on anything here.

Information can be incomplete or out of date

Programs, amounts, deadlines and funding rounds change often. The database was last reviewed November 11, 2026, and some programs couldn't be fully confirmed. Map data from public services can contain errors. Each program links to its official source; that source, not this site, is the authority.

Capital stack and pro forma estimates

The capital stack page uses simplified formulas and placeholder market numbers. Its tax credit math was compared with 13 real 2025-2026 awards in Oregon and Louisiana, but its full results haven't been checked against a real deal's complete sources and uses. Its results are rough estimates for learning and planning, not underwriting, appraisals, offers or commitments of funding. Real amounts depend on agency rules, competition, underwriting and market pricing.

The AI assistant can be wrong

The chatbot cites a source for every fact and is designed to say "I don't know" when unsure, but AI can still misread or misstate information. Check the cited sources before relying on an answer.

Links and third-party services

The site links to government and third-party websites it doesn't control and isn't responsible for. Those sites have their own terms and privacy practices.
