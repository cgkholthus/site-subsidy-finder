About

Making affordable housing funding easier to find.

Funding for affordable housing is spread across federal agencies, state housing finance agencies, cities and neighborhood districts. Develop Affordable pulls that research into one place for two cities, so a developer can start with an address instead of a dozen websites.

I built Develop Affordable as a student project after seeing how much time goes into the first question on any affordable housing deal: what funding could this site qualify for? Answering it usually means digging through federal maps, state allocation plans, city notices and district rules. This tool collects that research for New Orleans and Portland and connects it to a specific address.

What the site does

  • Subsidy Finder. Enter an address to see its federal designations (such as Qualified Census Tracts and Opportunity Zones), its local districts, and the programs that are potentially relevant there, each with an official source and an agency contact.
  • Capital Stack & Pro Forma. Rough out sources and uses and a 15-year operating pro forma, either from scratch or from a site's programs, and download it as a spreadsheet.
  • Ask a Question. A student-built assistant that explains programs and the estimate's math, cites a source for every fact, and says "I don't know" when the research doesn't cover something.

How it was built

The program information comes from a database I researched from official sources: federal agencies, state housing finance agencies, and city and neighborhood program pages. Each program lists its source and the date it was last verified. Site details come live from free public map services run by the U.S. Census Bureau, HUD, the National Park Service and the two cities.

There's no paid data and no per-search AI research. The question assistant runs on Google's Gemini AI and answers only from this site's research.

This is a student prototype. It's built for learning and early research, not as a professional or official service. Programs change often, and location alone never establishes eligibility. Always confirm with the agency that runs a program. Read the full disclaimers.
